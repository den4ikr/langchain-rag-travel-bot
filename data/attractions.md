# Budapest Travel Guide: Top Attractions

## 1. Hungarian Parliament Building

The Hungarian Parliament Building is one of the most recognizable landmarks in Budapest. It stands on the eastern bank of the Danube River and is famous for its Gothic Revival architecture.

**Location:** Kossuth Lajos Square, Budapest  
**Recommended visit duration:** 1–2 hours  
**Best for:** Architecture, history, photography

Visitors can explore the interior through guided tours. Booking tickets in advance is recommended, especially during busy tourist seasons.

**Travel tip:** Visit the riverbank in the evening to see the illuminated building.

## 2. Buda Castle

Buda Castle is a historic palace complex located on Castle Hill on the Buda side of the city. The surrounding district features cobblestone streets, historic buildings, museums, and panoramic views.

**Location:** Castle Hill, Budapest  
**Recommended visit duration:** 2–3 hours  
**Best for:** History, architecture, scenic views

Visitors can explore the castle district on foot and visit nearby attractions such as Matthias Church and Fisherman's Bastion.

**Travel tip:** Wear comfortable shoes because the district has steep streets and uneven surfaces.

## 3. Fisherman's Bastion

Fisherman's Bastion is a neo-Romanesque terrace offering panoramic views of the Danube, the Parliament Building, and Pest.

**Location:** Szentháromság Square, Budapest  
**Recommended visit duration:** 30–60 minutes  
**Best for:** Photography, couples, sightseeing

The terrace is particularly popular around sunrise and sunset. Some upper terrace sections may require a ticket, depending on the time and applicable access rules.

**Travel tip:** Arrive early in the morning to avoid the largest crowds.

## 4. St. Stephen's Basilica

St. Stephen's Basilica is one of Budapest's most important religious and architectural landmarks. Its interior contains elaborate decorations, and its panoramic terrace provides views across the city.

**Location:** Szent István Square, Budapest  
**Recommended visit duration:** 45–90 minutes  
**Best for:** Architecture, history, city views

Visitors should check current opening hours and any special access restrictions before visiting.

**Travel tip:** Combine the basilica with a walk through the nearby pedestrian streets and cafés.

## 5. Széchenyi Thermal Bath

Széchenyi Thermal Bath is one of Budapest's best-known thermal bathing complexes. It features outdoor and indoor pools in a historic Neo-Baroque building.

**Location:** City Park, Budapest  
**Recommended visit duration:** 2–4 hours  
**Best for:** Relaxation, wellness, unique experiences

Visitors can enjoy thermal pools and other bathing facilities. Available services, prices, and opening hours vary.

**Travel tip:** Bring swimwear, sandals, and anything else required by the current visitor rules. Check ticket availability in advance.

## 6. Heroes' Square

Heroes' Square is a large public square featuring statues of important figures from Hungarian history. It is located near City Park and the Museum of Fine Arts.

**Location:** Andrássy Avenue, Budapest  
**Recommended visit duration:** 20–40 minutes  
**Best for:** History, photography, walking tours

The square is an easy stop to combine with City Park, Vajdahunyad Castle, and Széchenyi Thermal Bath.

**Travel tip:** Visit early in the day if you want photographs with fewer people.

## 7. Great Market Hall

The Great Market Hall is a historic indoor market selling Hungarian food products, produce, spices, souvenirs, and other local goods.

**Location:** Vámház Boulevard, Budapest  
**Recommended visit duration:** 45–90 minutes  
**Best for:** Local food, shopping, cultural experiences

Visitors can explore traditional Hungarian ingredients and sample local specialties where available.

**Travel tip:** Check the market's current opening days and hours before planning your visit.

## 8. Margaret Island

Margaret Island is a green recreational area in the middle of the Danube. It offers walking paths, gardens, open spaces, and opportunities to relax away from busy city streets.

**Location:** Danube River, between Buda and Pest  
**Recommended visit duration:** 1–3 hours  
**Best for:** Nature, walking, cycling, families

The island is suitable for a leisurely walk or a break between sightseeing activities.

**Travel tip:** Allow extra time if you want to explore the island on foot rather than visit just one area.

## 9. Andrássy Avenue

Andrássy Avenue is a historic boulevard known for its architecture, shops, cafés, and cultural landmarks. It connects central Budapest with Heroes' Square.

**Location:** Central Pest  
**Recommended visit duration:** 1–2 hours  
**Best for:** Architecture, shopping, city walks

The avenue is a good route for exploring the city on foot. The Hungarian State Opera House is one of its notable landmarks.

**Travel tip:** Combine a walk along the avenue with a visit to Heroes' Square.

## 10. Shoes on the Danube Bank

Shoes on the Danube Bank is a memorial commemorating Jewish victims who were murdered along the river during the Second World War.

**Location:** Danube riverbank near the Parliament Building  
**Recommended visit duration:** 15–30 minutes  
**Best for:** History, remembrance, cultural education

The memorial consists of iron shoes placed along the riverbank. Visitors are encouraged to approach the site respectfully.

**Travel tip:** Combine the memorial with a walk along the Danube Promenade and a visit to the Parliament area.

## Sample One-Day Sightseeing Route

For a first visit to Budapest, a possible itinerary is:

1. Start at St. Stephen's Basilica.
2. Walk towards the Hungarian Parliament Building.
3. Visit the Shoes on the Danube Bank memorial.
4. Cross the Danube towards the Buda side.
5. Explore Buda Castle and Fisherman's Bastion.
6. Finish with dinner in the city centre.

This route prioritizes major landmarks and scenic walks. Actual travel times depend on walking speed, transport connections, ticket availability, and time spent at each attraction.

## General Travel Advice

- Verify opening hours, ticket prices, and access conditions before visiting.
- Reserve timed-entry attractions in advance when appropriate.
- Use comfortable walking shoes.
- Keep an eye on personal belongings in crowded tourist areas.
- Allow extra time for meals, public transport, and unexpected delays.
